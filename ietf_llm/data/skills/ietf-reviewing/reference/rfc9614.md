# Partitioning for privacy -- RFC 9614

**Text:** `rfc9614.txt`

## Scope

The *shape* of who-holds-what. Least privilege applied to user-specific information: §2's framing is
separating *who* someone is from *what* they do, so that no party other than the user holds both.

IAB-stream informational, and a survey as much as a recommendation. A finding cites the shape, not a
violated requirement.

**Not covered:** whether the data is sensitive, which is RFC 6973; whether the observation is at
monitoring scale, which is RFC 7258. §2.1 leans on RFC 6973's correlation material rather than
restating it, so a correlation finding usually belongs there.

## Firing

One party in the design learns more than it needs to do its job -- characteristically, a single party
holding both the user's identity and their activity.

### Versus only looking like it

The trigger reads as though it fires on almost any multi-party system, and it nearly does. What
separates a finding from an observation is whether a partitioning remedy is actually available:
recommending an oblivious transport for a deployed single-fetch protocol is redesign, not review.

**§6 is the brake.** Partitioning substantially changes communication patterns -- traffic routed
through intermediaries, added round trips, and the costs §4.4 describes when existing anti-abuse
systems depend on the very client identifier you would remove. Read it before asking for a
restructure.

Where no remedy is available, this lens still earns its place -- but the finding is *disclosure*, not
restructuring: the document should state the correlation rather than fix it. Say that explicitly, or
the recipient hears an impossible demand.

**More contexts is not automatically better.** §4.3 says splitting information across contexts
increases the number of entities that must be trusted not to collude. A finding that proposes a
partition should say who the new trusted party is.

## What this lens asks

- List each party and what it learns. Does any hold both identity and activity?
- Is that combination needed for the function, or incidental to how the design is arranged?
- Could the two be held by different parties without breaking anything -- and who would the new party
  be?
- Does any information in a context serve more purposes than it needs to?
- What happens if two parties collude?

## Where to look

**The framework**

- **§2** -- the least-privilege goal and the who/what framing.
- **§2.1 Privacy Contexts** -- a context is a set of data, metadata and the entities sharing access
  to it; the rule is that no single entity other than the client participates in more than one
  context where the information is visible. This is the test.
- **§2.2 Context Separation**, **§2.3 Approaches to Partitioning** -- how separation is achieved
  (cryptographic protection, connection separation) and what drives the choice: whether servers
  cooperate, whether the exchange must be interactive and bidirectional, and how much the client
  trusts each participant.

**The worked examples** -- §3, and the fastest way to find a structural analogue for the draft

- **§3.1 CONNECT Proxying and MASQUE** -- reach here for any proxying or tunnelling draft.
- **§3.2 Oblivious HTTP and DNS** -- the canonical case: DoH puts client address and query in one
  context; ODoH splits them into two.
- **§3.3 Privacy Pass**, **§3.4 Privacy Preserving Measurement** -- token issuance and
  one-directional metrics collection.

**Applying it** -- §4

- **§4.1 User-Identifying Information**, **§4.2 Selecting Client Identifiers**.
- **§4.3 Incorrect or Incomplete Partitioning** -- partitioning done badly, the collusion cost of
  more contexts, and the DoH-to-ODoH evaluation worked through.
- **§4.4 Selecting Information within a Context** -- information should serve as few purposes as
  possible, and the ossification problem when it already serves several.

**The limits** -- §5, where a proposed remedy gets tested

- **§5.1 Violations by Collusion** -- non-collusion is the fundamental requirement; without it
  partitioning buys nothing.
- **§5.2.1–§5.2.4** -- violations from application information, from network information, from side
  channels, and from partitions that are themselves identifying.

**The cost**

- **§6 Partitioning Impacts** -- what a restructure actually costs in routing and round trips.
